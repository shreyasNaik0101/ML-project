# Exploratory sensor traffic comparison

## Result

In this small exploratory test, the sensor-based traffic estimate did not improve the scores over the existing annual AADF feature. This is not evidence that the sensor data cannot help: the matches are not verified and the test includes only 90 crashes, including just one fatal crash. The 2024 cohort was previously used in project development, so this is not an untouched final evaluation.

| Measure on the same 90 crashes | AADF | Sensor estimate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Overall accuracy | 67.8% (61/90) | 66.7% (60/90) |
| Slight recall | 84.3% (59/70) | 82.9% (58/70) |
| Serious recall | 10.5% (2/19) | 10.5% (2/19) |
| Fatal recall | 0/1 | 0/1 |

Predicting Slight for every crash would achieve 77.8% accuracy on this small test, while finding no Serious or Fatal crashes. This illustrates why accuracy alone is misleading here. Serious and Fatal numbers are too small to provide reliable estimates. These scores are not directly comparable to previous project results because the cohort, training size and split differ.

## Matching and data quality

Among 100,927 2024 source crashes, the same-number, within-250-metre proximity rule found 1,795 candidates. Of those, 508 were also in the existing AADF-matched cohort: 404 Slight, 91 Serious and 13 Fatal. The candidate sensor may be on a parallel road or the opposite carriageway; the rule is not validated road-link matching.

For the 508 candidates, the collection returned a sufficiently recent, positive-speed pre-crash reading for 320. Another 180 had no eligible reading in their returned same-day data, six sensor-day requests failed after retries, and two had only stale eligible readings. These categories do not establish why the underlying reading was missing. Early-morning crashes requiring a previous-day reading were not recovered. The filtering and geographic coverage can affect the severity mix.

A coarse direction audit classified 189 of the 508 candidates as compatible, 189 as not compatible for at least one involved vehicle, 98 had a non-cardinal site direction (for example clockwise), and 32 had an unknown vehicle direction. The audit only checks broad compass directions, not a verified carriageway or continuous road geometry. Direction fields are recorded for the vehicles involved, so restricting a cohort using them is retrospective and can introduce selection bias. Among cases with a usable reading, this coarse direction-compatible subset had 80 training and 33 test crashes, including one Fatal in the test. The direction-compatible comparison was not run because the prespecified minimum of 100 training rows was not met.

## Experiment design

- Both models use the same collision rows and the existing seven-group feature representation and histogram gradient boosting settings. The only changed input is AADF versus `4 × quarter-hour count / average mph`, an approximate vehicles-per-mile measure across the monitored lanes, assuming count and speed are valid and applicable to the crash segment.
- Readings must finish at least 15 minutes before the recorded crash time and no more than 45 minutes earlier. The reported ending minute is treated as inclusive. Clock and time-zone conventions require further verification.
- January–September 2024 provides training and October–December provides testing. Training records at sites also present in the test set were removed; nearby sites can nevertheless be correlated. The comparison is exploratory and does not use the original model's tuned decision weights.
- The broad comparison uses 210 training and 90 test crashes after removing 20 training rows at test sites. It does not validate sensor direction or segment, and it uses the default prediction rule for both models.
- Data retrieval was restricted to the AADF overlap, without selecting by severity. Raw severity was used for auditing and evaluation, not as a feature. No external dataset was held back as a genuinely untouched final test.

## Recommendation

Do not replace AADF based on this pilot. First improve road-link and carriageway matching, check historical sensor validity, interval quality and time zones, fetch prior-day readings when necessary, and establish a substantially larger matched cohort across years. Compare both approaches on the same matched rows and keep a fresh future dataset sealed until the entire pipeline is fixed. The larger data collection may still yield too few fatal crashes for a precise fatal-recall estimate.

Reproducible scripts: `audit_candidates.py`, `collect_readings.py`, and `compare_sensor_traffic.py`. The latter two rely on candidate locations generated by `work/traffic_pilot/find_crashes.py` and downloaded sensor catalogue and reading files under `work/traffic_pilot/`. Detailed numbers and confusion matrices are in `candidate_audit.json` and `sensor_comparison.json`.
